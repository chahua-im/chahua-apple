## Grouping and read receipts

iOS and macOS group consecutive non-system messages from the same sender within a calendar day, regardless of the time between messages. A date separator, unread separator, system message, or sender change starts a new group. The sender name appears on the first bubble of a group where sender names are enabled; the avatar appears on the final bubble. Base vertical gaps are 4 points within a group and 8 points between groups. Plain headerless text bubbles size to their contents rather than the avatar height; their single-line text is centered using painted glyph bounds, with the inline timestamp kept lower. A row reserves any extra avatar clearance outside a compact bubble so avatars cannot overlap neighboring rows.

Outgoing published messages show a single checkmark when sent and double checkmarks when read by the DM peer. Receipts are reciprocal and server-authorized; group conversations do not show read receipts. Main-conversation and thread read positions are independent, including the main-conversation root displayed in a thread. Scoped `readReceiptChanged` events refresh only the affected peer cursor; self-reader advances are ignored, while privacy revocation clears every affected cached scope immediately. Requests coalesce and stale responses cannot restore revoked receipts. Healthy foreground activation retains successful receipts; reconnects and failed queries still refresh.

**Settings → Privacy and Security → Read Receipts** controls sharing with friends or nobody. The account-wide Read Receipts screen uses radio rows on macOS and checkmarked rows on iOS, with its explanation in the grouped form footer or below the standalone settings card, followed by an **Exceptions → Never Share With** “Coming soon” list placeholder. A friend's DM information/profile page instead has a **Privacy Settings** navigation row. Its grouped page contains **Read Receipts** and **Last Seen & Online**, each offering **Use global setting**, **Share with this friend**, and **Hide from this friend**. Gray group footers explain reciprocity: hiding read receipts also hides the friend's receipts; hiding presence also prevents seeing their exact last-seen/online status. Choices save automatically; failed loads and saves offer Retry. The presence override uses the backend's `GET/PUT /friends/{uid}/presence-settings` API, with `inherit`, `share`, and `hide` values. Committed presence changes invalidate cached individual presence and refresh its watch.

Account-wide Read Receipts and Last Seen & Online use [explicit-save editing](settings-editing.md): selections stay local until Save succeeds. macOS split editors use a dirty-only bottom Revert/Save bar; iPad keeps Save top-right. Split-layout saves remain on the selected page. Compact iOS uses toolbar Save and guards dirty Back/swipe with discard confirmation. Per-friend overrides retain instant-save behavior.

Manual verification: with two friend accounts allowing receipt sharing, send a DM from Apple and open it in the PWA; the sent checkmark should become double. Repeat with a thread reply and confirm reading the parent conversation alone does not mark the reply read. Hide sharing on either account and confirm double checkmarks disappear; restore sharing, reconnect/foreground the Apple client, and confirm authorized receipts return. Group messages should remain single-checkmarked.

Privacy UI verification: open a friend's DM information page and confirm it shows a Privacy Settings row with a white hand on a blue rounded-square icon rather than inline read-receipt choices. Open it and check both groups, selected checkmarks, and gray reciprocity footers on iOS and macOS. Change each group, leave and reopen, and confirm the overrides persist independently; Use global setting restores inheritance.

## Presence display

Individual presence follows PWA precedence: **Online**, **Away**, a server-provided coarse `lastSeenBucket`, then an exact `lastSeenAt` timestamp. Coarse buckets are **recently**, **within a week**, **within a month**, and **a long time ago**; the client never derives an exact timestamp from a privacy-filtered bucket. A known presence record without any status uses the long-ago label; an unknown/pending snapshot stays undisplayed.

Exact last seen clamps future timestamps to just now. Under one minute shows **just now**, under one hour shows whole minutes, and under twelve hours shows whole hours. Older activity uses **today** or **yesterday** with 24-hour time, then a localized abbreviated month/day and time; the year is included only for activity in a different calendar year. Calendar-day comparisons use the local time zone, including daylight-saving transitions. The same presentation feeds the DM header and information/profile page.

## Friend safety actions

DM information/profile pages keep **Remove friend** and **Block/Unblock** inside **More**, alongside the group page's existing **Leave group** pattern. The actions are destructive/red and require an explicit confirmation naming the target; Cancel makes no request. Remove friend is offered only for an authoritative current friendship and closes the conversation on success. Block/Unblock reloads the relationship in place so its label and DM authorization reflect the server response. Self-targeted actions are disabled.

Committed mutations invalidate cached receipts, individual presence, and conversation lists. A failed post-mutation relationship refresh retries only the refresh, not the mutation that already succeeded. Manual verification: cancel each confirmation without a state change, then block/unblock a friend and confirm the menu and DM send permission update; remove a friend and confirm the conversation closes and can no longer be sent to.

## Delivery indicators and timestamps

Timeline delivery indicators are native `NSView`/`UIView` renderers; they no longer retain SwiftUI hosts or render offscreen SwiftUI snapshots. `MessageDeliveryStatusView.swift` keeps the standalone SwiftUI reference/preview alongside shared immutable status data and check geometry. Queued messages show a clock, sending messages use a native `CAShapeLayer` spinner, sent messages show a single check, and read messages show nested double checks with default separation 0.3. The check ink matches the timestamp digit height and baseline without changing spinner size or the reserved status slot. Timestamps retain native drawing and retry targets retain their existing interaction/accessibility behavior. All outgoing states reserve the same status width to avoid timestamp and text reflow; hiding, unmounting, or reusing a native indicator stops its animation.

Chat-list previews show the outgoing message's delivery indicator immediately before the timestamp, using the same status renderer as the timeline. A saved draft hides that indicator. Incoming, system, and deleted message previews do not show it; double checks require an authorized receipt for that exact DM or DM-thread scope.

Manual verification: send a message while the list is visible, then open it from the peer account and confirm the list changes from a single to a double check. Repeat for a DM thread and a group, and confirm the group never shows double checks. Save a draft and confirm it replaces the preview without a delivery indicator; remove the draft to restore the message status.

Bubble timestamps use the smaller caption style (11 points at the standard iOS text size), with a 2-point timestamp/status gap. Outgoing timestamps are muted to 60% opacity while checks remain fully opaque; media overlays use 85% timestamp opacity for legibility. The footer extends 2 points lower and farther right than the body text at the default timestamp size, scaling with text size. Body wrapping retains its original width, and native text views install that body width independently from the footer's full drawing bounds.

When a text-only bubble's timestamp needs its own row, its width contracts to the actual text/footer and any header, reply, or thread content instead of retaining the failed inline reservation. Compaction cannot add a body line; media bubbles retain their media-driven width. Widening the timeline restores inline timestamps when they fit.

On macOS, the native text view saves/restores graphics state around `NSTextView.draw` before painting its timestamp. AppKit leaves a clip at the narrower body container; carrying it into footer drawing cuts off the final digit in the trailing outset. This isolation preserves the footer's full ink without widening body wrapping or changing bubble padding. A native bitmap regression covers an incoming CJK/emoji message with `00:48`.

The workshop renders the production Core Text timestamp and fitted status frame, with controls for timestamp size, spinner stroke, check separation, symbol weight, and a **Play** sequence from queued through read. Open **Message status workshop** in the Debug component gallery or launch with `-message-status-preview`; neither route starts authenticated services.

## Reactions, selection, and forwarding

Message actions offer **Reactions** for published messages with reactions. On iOS and macOS, the sheet loads the full reactor list and uses horizontally scrollable emoji/count tabs with an accent underline above compact avatar/name rows. **All** groups reactions by person; up to eight populated emoji groups get individual tabs, and **More** contains the remaining populated groups. Zero-reaction groups are omitted; an entirely empty response shows the empty state. Reactor rows do not open profiles. Loading failures offer Retry.

The quick-reaction **+** replaces the reaction strip and action menu with a native smiley placeholder and full Unicode emoji picker, while keeping the selected message highlighted. macOS uses `TimelineReactionPickerView` with AppKit search, paging/category controls, scroll views, buttons, and tone menus. iOS uses `NativeReactionEmojiKeyboard` with UIKit search, a collection view, category controls, and tone menus. Both share the bundled catalog, search index, recents, eligibility, and mutation controller; they do not embed SwiftUI or substitute the system emoji panel. Search supports English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, and exact pasted sequences. Category buttons and horizontal pages browse the catalog; the clock shows recent choices. Long-press/right-click an emoji with variants to select its skin tone. Close, the backdrop, and Escape cancel without changing reactions.

The app bundles `App/Resources/reaction-emoji-17.json`, generated from pinned Unicode 17 data by `python3 scripts/generate-reaction-emoji.py`, and the accompanying Unicode license. No third-party picker package or runtime catalog request is used. Explicit fully-qualified sequences preserve Unicode ordering and mixed-tone variants. Catalog loading caches installed-font support checks and excludes unsupported/decomposed emoji rather than showing broken compositions.

Picker verification: open a message menu and press **+**; confirm both old menus disappear, the selected message remains identifiable, and the smiley/keyboard appear. Browse categories, swipe pages, choose a toned emoji, and confirm exactly one reaction toggles and the picker closes. Reopen and cancel with Close, the backdrop, and Escape; no reaction should change. At five personal or fifty distinct reactions, permitted removals/existing reactions remain reachable. Check portrait/landscape, light/dark appearance, VoiceOver/keyboard navigation, and deletion or permission loss while open.

On iOS, `MessageInteractionWindowPresenter` installs a native controller in the source window. Its native bubble preview, quick reactions, action grid, and full picker do not use `UIHostingController` or create a replacement key window. Replacing or dismissing a target tears down its preview and keyboard state; a replacement arriving during dismissal is attached after the old controller is removed. macOS jump-to-latest, unread-mention, and unread-reaction controls are native AppKit buttons with the same count, busy, and accessibility state. Voice-message playback remains the explicit nested SwiftUI hosting exception inside timeline components.

On iOS and macOS, message actions include **Forward** and **Select**. Selection captures up to 100 nondeleted, nonsystem messages with server IDs, including acknowledged audio still processing; invites and nested records remain selectable but are validated when forwarding. Tap or click a row to toggle its right-side circle/checkmark. The composer is replaced by Delete, count, Cancel, and Forward controls; zero disables Forward. Bulk deletion requires write permission and ownership/admin authority for every selected message, runs sequentially, and retains uncompleted selections on failure. Selection survives pagination, preserves the composer draft, and clears when leaving the conversation. Links, media, reactions, and reply gestures are inactive while selecting.

Selection toolbar actions use explicit colors: Cancel is always primary blue; enabled Forward is blue and enabled Delete is red. Empty selections and in-progress/unauthorized actions remain disabled grey. macOS Escape invokes the same Cancel action, including cancellation of an active selection-delete task. Verify zero/nonzero selections, mixed delete permissions, and Escape while selecting; the composer draft must remain intact.

Forwarding one message preserves its content type with flattened source attribution and an inert original-reply preview; stickers and forwarded-message cards omit that extra attribution. Forwarded text cannot be edited. Forwarding multiple messages creates one **Chat history** card with a bold heading, up to three author summaries in secondary text, a divider, and a single footer containing the total count on the left and timestamp/delivery state on the right. The native iOS and macOS cards share cached geometry so the footer cannot overlap its previews or count. Existing incoming/outgoing bubble colors are retained.

On iPhone, iPad, and macOS, forwarding stays in one modal: a Close control and source/message-count subtitle sit above inline search, **Saved Messages** appears first, and **Recent** mixes nonarchived chats and subscribed threads by activity with independent paging/retry. Choosing a destination advances to its message preview inside the same modal; **Back** or **Change Destination** returns to the picker without sending. Only explicit **Forward** creates the destination's durable outbox operation or saves to Saved Messages. Success closes the modal and completes source selection with the existing forwarding toast; cancellation or failure leaves source selection intact. No destination navigation or composer staging occurs. Text, reply, and attachment drafts in both conversations remain untouched; the destination's existing outbox owns ordering, confirmation, failure, and stable-key retries.

The macOS sheet explicitly reserves a 420×560pt destination viewport; a flexible scroll view cannot supply a reliable ideal height to AppKit on its own. The preview uses a shorter count-dependent height. iPhone previews offer a smaller detent plus expansion to large; iPad/macOS use trailing dialog actions while compact iPhone uses a full-width Forward action. Preview messages retain the existing incoming/outgoing palette. Saving a multi-selection captures each source through the existing Saved Messages API; it never toggles an already-saved item off, and retrying after a partial save retains completed captures.

Opening a card pushes a **Forwarded Messages** page onto the conversation's existing navigation stack; nested cards push another page. ## Unread activity and realtime state

Unread activity mirrors the PWA: `@` combines mentions and replies to your messages in groups and group threads; a heart marks unread reactions in any conversation. Chat/thread rows show these symbols beside the unread-message badge, muted or archived rows use subdued badges, and category tabs prioritize `@` using server-wide summaries rather than loaded rows.

Both production timelines share `TimelineNavigationControls`: stacked heart/`@` buttons above jump-to-latest, with counts capped visually at `999+`. Each click visits the oldest remaining target. Session targets survive counter clearing; missing/deleted targets are skipped and transient navigation failures remain retryable. Main chats and threads keep independent queues. Entry and reconnects refresh activity; healthy visibility restoration reuses successful snapshots and known-zero counters. Directed realtime changes coalesce follow-up reads without losing changes received during a request.

Mentions/replies clear through the existing visible-message read cursor. Reactions use a separate cursor: foreground entry acknowledges the exact queried snapshot boundary, while foreground navigation away acknowledges the conversation once more. Backgrounding does not acknowledge reactions; cancelled or old-account responses cannot revive queues or issue late acknowledgements.

Active timelines automatically remove visible activity targets after server read confirmation, including activity arriving while the viewport is stationary. Mentions wait for the successful message-read cursor (or an authoritative cursor already covering that message); reactions wait for the exact fetched snapshot's successful acknowledgement. Reaction acknowledgement is snapshot-wide, not a per-message API; offscreen navigation targets remain available even after that server counter clears. Only fresh, settled, unobscured viewport IDs qualify; background, stale and pending-scroll viewports cannot dismiss targets. Later reactions on the same old message can create a new target.

Regression coverage holds the message-read response open to verify that visible mentions persist until confirmation, checks the exact reaction snapshot acknowledgement, retains offscreen targets, excludes inactive visibility and allows fresh activity on an old message. The 90 targeted timeline/chrome tests passed; the final activity regression also passed after acknowledgement-set cleanup. Live two-client verification remains manual: receive activity with its target visible, then repeat offscreen and in a thread; only the visible confirmed targets should disappear.

Unread entry reveals the frozen unread separator at the top of the unobscured viewport, clamped to the live bottom when there is insufficient content below it. Settled initial layout reports read visibility without requiring a scroll gesture. `TimelineViewport.visibleMessageIDs` contains confirmed rows intersecting the area between header and composer, in timeline order: partially visible and taller-than-viewport messages count; wholly obscured/offscreen and pending rows do not. The first eligible visible cursor is acknowledged immediately; later candidates retain the 500 ms stable-visibility dwell, read-watermark checks, and lifecycle/account fencing. Opening alone must never acknowledge an unseen latest message.

Read-entry verification: open a long chat with one or a few unread messages at the bottom without scrolling, then repeat with tall media. With a larger unread batch, verify top alignment and that offscreen newer messages remain unread. Repeat in threads and after background/foreground; a backwards scroll must not move the cursor backwards.

Manual verification: from a second account, mention you and reply to one of your messages in a group, then react to one of your messages. Confirm `@`/heart row badges and the tab's `@` priority; open the group and use both jump buttons until their targets are exhausted. Repeat in a group thread and confirm parent activity stays independent. In a DM/thread, reactions should work without an `@` button. Background and reconnect Apple while new activity arrives, then confirm foregrounding restores the targets. Repeat with muted and archived conversations and verify subdued row badges without an active unmuted tab-attention badge.

Unread tab/archive summaries and the app-icon badge come from `watchConversationUnread`, not the removed HTTP summary endpoint or a sum of loaded pages. Visible list rows and open timelines share `watchConversationStates` (up to 512 distinct chat/thread scopes). Absolute full and partial snapshots update counters and read cursors; request IDs fence old subscriptions/accounts, while scoped versions fence read acknowledgements without restarting healthy watches. Known read cursors suppress equal or older acknowledgements even outside the loaded message window. Unwatched scopes or unavailable state snapshots use the chat/thread read-state GET endpoints; an ambiguous lower cursor after a local acknowledgement uses a scope-only read to resolve a possible mark-unread or deletion rewind. Background scenes release watches; foreground scenes replace them with fresh request IDs. A healthy socket retains event-maintained lists and message windows; actual reconnects reconcile them. See [visible-message read progress](realtime-messaging.md#visible-message-read-progress) for watermark and rewind behavior.

The timeline back-button badge is deliberately approximate and separate from authoritative read, tab, and app-icon counters. A server total seeds a retained baseline, including chats outside loaded list pages. Deduplicated eligible main-chat message broadcasts adjust local per-chat estimates; authoritative chat snapshots and read acknowledgements replace their contributions. The displayed route excludes its chat (or thread parent), independently per scene, so arrivals and read acknowledgements in that chat cannot flash the back badge. Known navigation and message arrivals do not request a filtered recount. Reconnects and explicit uncertainty use coalesced temporary unread-watch views excluding the visible chats; those views are removed after calibration. Snapshot payloads have no event watermark, so calibration replaces estimates without replaying raced events. Ordinary drift is accepted until a per-chat correction or explicit calibration rather than triggering recounts from differences between independently delivered global and local counters.

Message events update known previews directly. Membership, archive, and deletion reconciliation use targeted `chatId`/`rootId` list queries rather than reloading unrelated pages. Thread membership events carry absolute subscription/archive status; ordinary reply updates do not invalidate a known subscription.

On iOS, conversation lists extend their scrolling viewport to the bottom edge rather than clipping rows above the home-indicator area. Bottom clearance lives inside the scroll content, allowing the last row to scroll fully above that area and any voice playback bar. The shell passes its bottom safe-area inset through navigation, whose hidden-bar stack otherwise resets the playback clearance; macOS layout and timeline composer insets remain unchanged.

Manual verification: read a chat and a thread in the PWA while Apple shows their list rows; confirm counts and the system badge update without switching tabs. Send messages while Apple is backgrounded, return with a healthy connection, and confirm previews/targets remain available without a full-list loading cycle. Repeat after a real disconnect, archive/unarchive and subscribe/unsubscribe from the PWA, then switch accounts while updates are pending; old snapshots must not restore counts or memberships.

## Image viewer

The image viewer header shows Back, the chat/thread name above the message timestamp, and an overflow menu containing only **Save Image**. iOS requests add-only Photos access and saves the original image file; macOS uses a native Save panel with user-selected read/write access. Saving has no progress/status message, prevents duplicate requests, and reports success or failure with a transient accessible toast. Canceling the Save panel is silent. Video saving is not included.

On macOS the image viewer and sidebar account/new-chat controls share the SwiftUI content layer. The viewer naturally covers those controls and suppresses underlying interaction/accessibility; no viewer-specific toolbar visibility flag or `zIndex` workaround is needed. The native title-band toolbar stays visible with a hidden background, preserving the standard traffic lights and edge-to-edge content. Sidebar controls reserve the native buttons with ordinary padding rather than custom draggable-region handling.

A temporary SwiftUI `WindowGroup` smoke used the app's hidden-title-bar and unified-toolbar scene configuration. Its whole-window captures showed all three native controls visible, with content extending to the top edge and the account/actions covered by the overlay. Native hit tests at both content-control positions reached the SwiftUI window hosting view before, during and after the overlay; window/button geometry stayed fixed. A manually constructed hosting window is not an adequate substitute for checking this scene behavior. Physical menu clicks and window-drag feel remain manual checks.

Restoration verification: the existing native-window regression now applies the production chrome modifier and checks traffic-light visibility/alpha as well as position through conversation switches and resize. It passed (`/tmp/chahua-window-chrome-restored.xcresult`), and `./format.sh --check` passed. The temporary scene-smoke source and executable were removed.

Video playback downloads a complete movie through `AppMediaContext.videoCache` before creating a local-file `AVPlayerItem`. Downloads are shared per URL, byte progress is determinate only for consistent identity-encoded lengths, and cancellation removes a consumer without interrupting other viewers. Completed, playable files use a separate 512 MiB least-recently-used disk cache. Leases pin playing files; clear/invalidate records a durable discard marker and removes the file after its last lease. Oversized active movies may temporarily exceed the limit. Settings includes image and movie storage in its cache accounting.

`VideoPoster` shares a 512-pixel Kingfisher data-provider representation between native message bubbles and the chat-info album. Poster generation is cancellable on reuse/disappearance and does not insert a full movie into the playback cache. Images/videos use square album tiles; their source-message navigation is a native context-menu/VoiceOver action rather than a filename row.

The progress ring has UIKit/AppKit wrappers because the viewer pages are native surfaces. It displays real byte percentage when available and an indeterminate ring otherwise; only the selected foreground page owns download/playback work.

iOS movie audio uses per-page ownership and one non-main serial queue for `AVAudioSession` category/activation/deactivation. The SDK's asynchronous activation APIs require iOS 27, while the app supports iOS 17; the queue keeps the blocking compatibility calls off the UI thread without two lifecycle implementations. Queued work rereads current ownership, so closing/reopening during activation cannot leave a stale session active or deactivate another viewer. Failed deactivation retains active bookkeeping for a later cleanup attempt.

Audio-session verification: open/play a movie, rapidly close and reopen it, switch selected pages, background/foreground, and exit fullscreen. Playback should resume only for the selected foreground page; closing one viewer must not silence another owner. Check physical-device audio routing/interruption behavior separately from simulator ownership smoke.
